<ContentSplit eyebrow="Product Design" title="Data Sync, made simple">

<SplitColumn>

<Detail title="Overview + Role">

The Data Sync Configuration page controls how and when customer data arrives from platforms like QuickBooks and Xero, but new customers couldn't set it up on their own and relied on Customer Success to get started. I redesigned the page around how customers actually set up, and added the dashboard's first guided walkthrough, working alongside a product manager.

</Detail>

<Detail title="The problem">

New customers couldn't understand the page, and they couldn't make it do what they needed.

<Points spacing="loose">

<Point title="Hard to understand">

Two kinds of sync, layered settings, and sandbox vs. production, with nothing on the page explaining any of it.

</Point>

<Point title="Hard to control">

Every customer needed different data, at a different frequency, for different reasons.

</Point>

</Points>

</Detail>

</SplitColumn>

<SplitColumn>

<UserStories intro="The user stories showed how wide the range was:">

<UserStory title="Fintech Platform" text="Wanted to choose which endpoints sync for each platform." />
<UserStory title="Enterprise Bank" text="Wanted to request only transaction data, not full financials, so more users would agree to connect." />
<UserStory title="Expense Management" text="Needed only transactions and vendors, to keep costs down." />
<UserStory title="Payments & Consent" text="Needed to be blocked from data its end users hadn't consented to share." />
<UserStory title="Accounting Automation" text="Wanted to sync less often to cut API costs." />
<UserStory title="Lending & Underwriting" text="Needed financial statements for underwriting, but no real-time transaction sync." />

</UserStories>

</SplitColumn>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="The walkthrough" title="A guided first run" image="/images/rutter-guided-first-run.gif" magnify="true" imageAlt="Animated walkthrough of the redesigned Data Sync Configuration page: the guided tour spotlights each section in turn, starting with Initial Historical Sync, while the rest of the page is dimmed and numbered markers 1 to 4 show the steps" imageWidth="1960" imageHeight="1208" side="left">

<Detail title="The solution">

<Points>

<Point title="Spotlight focus">

The active section is highlighted and the rest of the page is dimmed.

</Point>

<Point title="Persistent markers">

Numbered steps stay on the page and pulse when they appear, so customers always know where they are.

</Point>

<Point title="Skippable, not lost">

Customers can skip the tour and reopen it from the page whenever they want.

</Point>

<Point title="No dead ends">

Back, Continue and a progress bar on every step.

</Point>

</Points>

</Detail>

</MediaSplit>

<SubSection>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="Information Architecture" title="Three levels of control" image="/images/rutter-three-levels-of-control.webp" magnify="true" imageAlt="Data Sync Configuration page with the QuickBooks Settings side panel open: platform sync frequency inherited from the organization default of every 6 hours, sync status and history, and per-object toggles for Account, Bills, Customers, Invoices and Payments" imageWidth="2379" imageHeight="1466" side="right">

<Detail title="The solution">

<Points>

<Point title="Inherit by default">

Set the schedule once for the whole organization, and every platform and object follows it automatically.

</Point>

<Point title="Override where it matters">

Each platform shows a "Default" or "Custom" badge, so customers can see at a glance which settings are their own.

</Point>

<Point title="Edit in context">

Side panels open a single platform or object without losing sight of the rest of the hierarchy.

</Point>

<Point title="Safe to change">

Changing the org default asks for confirmation first, because it affects everything that inherits it, and "Reset to org default" undoes any override.

</Point>

</Points>

</Detail>

</MediaSplit>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="Added control" title="When full history isn't enough" image="/images/rutter-initial-sync-batching.gif" magnify="true" imageAlt="Animated walkthrough of the Initial Sync Batching side panel: a short guided tour shows picking a category, setting a batch's time frame and objects, adding a pre-curated bucket like All Accounts Payable objects, and saving, with four batches listed" imageWidth="1960" imageHeight="1208" side="left">

<Detail title="The solution">

In testing, heavy objects held up everything else from reaching "ready." I added batching so customers can give slow objects their own batch and time frame, and gave the panel its own short tour using the same walkthrough pattern.

</Detail>

<Detail title="The outcome">

<Points>

<Point title="Self-serve onboarding">

New customers learn the page from the page itself. The walkthrough replaces the setup call.

</Point>

<Point title="Control for every use case">

Organization, platform and object settings cover all six customer types we started with, from consent limits to cost cutting.

</Point>

<Point title="Faster first sync">

Batching keeps heavy objects from holding up the rest, so customers get usable data sooner.

</Point>

<Point title="CS time back">

Fewer setup calls frees the Customer Success team for higher-value work.

</Point>

</Points>

</Detail>
