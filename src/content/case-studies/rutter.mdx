<ContentSplit eyebrow="Product Design" title="Data Sync, made simple">

<SplitColumn>

<Detail title="Overview + Role">

Rutter is a unified API that connects B2B software to the accounting, commerce, ads and banking platforms their customers use, like QuickBooks, Xero and NetSuite. The Data Sync Configuration page controls how and when that data arrives, but new customers couldn't set it up on their own and relied on Customer Success to get started.

I redesigned the page around how customers actually set up, and added the dashboard's first guided walkthrough, working alongside a product manager.

</Detail>

<Detail title="The problem">

New customers couldn't understand the page, and they couldn't make it do what they needed.

- **Hard to understand:** two kinds of sync, layered settings, and sandbox vs. production, with nothing on the page explaining any of it.
- **Hard to control:** every customer needed different data, at a different frequency, for different reasons.

</Detail>

</SplitColumn>

<SplitColumn>

<SubSection>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="Part one: the walkthrough" title="A guided first run" image="/images/rutter-guided-first-run.gif" imageAlt="Animated walkthrough of the redesigned Data Sync Configuration page: the guided tour spotlights each section in turn, starting with Initial Historical Sync, while the rest of the page is dimmed and numbered markers 1 to 4 show the steps" imageWidth="1960" imageHeight="1208" side="left">

<Detail title="The solution">

- **Spotlight focus:** the active section is highlighted and the rest of the page is dimmed.
- **Persistent markers:** numbered steps stay on the page and pulse when they appear, so customers always know where they are.
- **Skippable, not lost:** customers can skip the tour and reopen it from the page whenever they want.
- **No dead ends:** Back, Continue and a progress bar on every step.

</Detail>

</MediaSplit>

</SubSection>

<SubSection>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="Part two: the information architecture" title="Three levels of control" image="/images/rutter-three-levels-of-control.webp" imageAlt="Data Sync Configuration page with the QuickBooks Settings side panel open: platform sync frequency inherited from the organization default of every 6 hours, sync status and history, and per-object toggles for Account, Bills, Customers, Invoices and Payments" imageWidth="2379" imageHeight="1466" side="right">

<Detail title="The solution">

- **Inherit by default:** set the schedule once for the whole organization, and every platform and object follows it automatically.
- **Override where it matters:** each platform shows a "Default" or "Custom" badge, so customers can see at a glance which settings are their own.
- **Edit in context:** side panels open a single platform or object without losing sight of the rest of the hierarchy.
- **Safe to change:** changing the org default asks for confirmation first, because it affects everything that inherits it, and "Reset to org default" undoes any override.

</Detail>

</MediaSplit>

</SubSection>

<SubSection>

<MediaSplit eyebrow="Added control" title="When full history isn't enough" image="/images/rutter-initial-sync-batching.gif" imageAlt="Animated walkthrough of the Initial Sync Batching side panel: a short guided tour shows picking a category, setting a batch's time frame and objects, adding a pre-curated bucket like All Accounts Payable objects, and saving, with four batches listed" imageWidth="1960" imageHeight="1208" side="left">

<Detail title="The solution">

In testing, heavy objects held up everything else from reaching "ready." I added batching so customers can give slow objects their own batch and time frame, and gave the panel its own short tour using the same walkthrough pattern.

</Detail>

</MediaSplit>

<SubSection>

<ContentSplit eyebrow="Results" title="The outcome">

<SplitColumn>

<Detail title="What changed">

- **Self-serve onboarding:** new customers learn the page from the page itself. The walkthrough replaces the setup call.
- **Control for every use case:** organization, platform and object settings cover all six customer types we started with, from consent limits to cost cutting.
- **Faster first sync:** batching keeps heavy objects from holding up the rest, so customers get usable data sooner.
- **CS time back:** fewer setup calls frees the Customer Success team for higher-value work.

</Detail>

</SplitColumn>

<SplitColumn>

<Detail title="Next time?">

I'd track tour completion and skip rates from launch to tie the walkthrough directly to fewer support requests.

</Detail>
