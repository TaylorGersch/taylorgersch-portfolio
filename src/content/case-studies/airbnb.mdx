<TwoCol
  overview="Organizing Cases was one of the flagship pieces of Project Atrium — a full redesign of the case inbox every support ambassador used to manage their queue. I worked with a product manager to understand ambassador pain points and conducted several ambassador interviews for their feedback."
  problem="Ambassadors couldn't easily tell what actually needed their attention. Cases lived in a single flat list with no reliable way to distinguish a brand-new case from one still waiting on a guest or host reply, so ambassadors were opening cases one by one just to figure out where to focus — a slow way to work through a queue that could run dozens of cases deep."
/>

<Pair>

<Detail title="The process">

I ran a full audit of every case UI-state that needed defining — brand new and unopened, a guest or host reply that hadn't been read yet, one that had been read, one an ambassador had already responded to. From there, I worked within a two-week sprint to define exactly how each state should look and behave: what stayed bold, what showed a status dot, how a card looked on hover versus once selected. Getting this system right mattered more than any single screen, since ambassadors were scanning this list dozens of times a day.

</Detail>

<Detail title="The solution">

A rebuilt case inbox split into Open and Pending tabs, with a consistent visual hierarchy across every case state so a glance shows exactly where attention is needed first. The case-preview fly-out was a net-new feature, surfacing a case's reservation details and message thread on hover so ambassadors could pick up right where they left off. Two CTAs, Set to pending and Open case, were the main actions available from that window.

</Detail>

</Pair>

<div className="border-t border-neutral-200 pt-8">

<Detail title="The outcome">

The redesigned inbox, along with the visual language behind it, shipped with the Copper release, giving ambassadors a way to understand a case's status at a glance instead of opening it to find out. It became the default way every Airbnb ambassador worked their queue.

</Detail>

</div>
