<TwoCol
  overview="Organizing Cases was one of the flagship pieces of Project Atrium — a full redesign of the case inbox every support ambassador used to manage their queue."
  problem="Ambassadors couldn't easily tell what actually needed their attention. Cases lived in a single flat list with no reliable way to distinguish a brand-new case from one still waiting on a guest or host reply, so ambassadors were opening cases one by one just to figure out where to focus — a slow way to work through a queue that could run dozens of cases deep."
/>

<Pair>

<Detail title="The process">

I interviewed ambassadors and partnered with the product manager to understand where the current inbox was falling short, then ran a full audit of every case UI-state that needed defining — brand new and unopened, a guest or host reply that hadn't been read yet, one that had been read, one an ambassador had already responded to. From there, I worked within a two-week sprint to define exactly how each state should look and behave: what stayed bold, what showed a status dot, how a card looked on hover versus once selected. Getting this system right mattered more than any single screen, since ambassadors were scanning this list dozens of times a day.

</Detail>

<Detail title="The solution">

A rebuilt case inbox split into Open and Pending tabs, so ambassadors could separate cases needing action from ones waiting on someone else, with a consistent visual hierarchy across every case state so a glance at the list shows exactly where attention is needed first. The case-preview fly-out was a net-new feature: hovering a case surfaced its reservation details and message thread without opening it, giving ambassadors a true at-a-glance context-gathering moment to pick up right where they — or another ambassador — left off. Two CTAs, Set to pending and Open case, were the main actions available directly from that window.

</Detail>

</Pair>

<div className="border-t border-neutral-200 pt-8">

<Detail title="The outcome">

The redesigned inbox, along with the visual language behind it, shipped with the Copper release, giving ambassadors a way to understand a case's status at a glance instead of opening it to find out. It became the default way every Airbnb ambassador worked their queue.

</Detail>
